## Daily Report — 2026-06-17

**Project:** LoadLens
**Author:** Bogdan Bucataru

1. Designed a capability that builds continuous load chains by drawing on freight shared across the whole network of connected brokers, not just a single dispatcher's current view.
2. Solved the problem of chains breaking when the destination market is empty by letting the planner pick up the next load from a nearby market within a short detour, keeping drivers moving with minimal extra mileage.
3. Designed a freshness mechanism that recognises when a load has likely already been booked and quietly drops it, so suggested chains no longer lead to dead ends.
4. Built the back-end service that returns available loads from a market together with its neighbouring markets, each annotated with how far away and how fresh it is.
5. Added load-popularity tracking so the system can infer which loads are still genuinely available across many dispatcher sessions.
6. Enabled the route planner to extend chains through neighbouring markets while keeping the added empty-miles to a minimum.
7. Built a live-monitoring mode so that, while a dispatcher keeps the panel open, suggestions refresh on their own and loads that disappear from the market are removed in near real time.
8. Reviewed every change across multiple independent quality and correctness passes before release.
9. Fixed an issue where chain suggestions could temporarily vanish after switching the active driver, and another where a re-listed load stayed hidden after briefly disappearing.
10. Verified the full automated test suite passed and integrated the completed feature, then released it to production and confirmed the live service was healthy.
11. Surfaced a freshness indicator on each forecast leg (fresh / cooling / likely gone) so dispatchers can judge at a glance how trustworthy a suggested load is.
12. Marked legs that come from a neighbouring market with a small "detour" tag, making it clear when a chain steps into an adjacent area to stay continuous.
13. Reviewed the display changes for correctness and quality, released them to production, and confirmed the live service was healthy.
