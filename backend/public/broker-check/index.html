    Before you book a load from a new broker

    1. Call back on the phone number in the FMCSA record, not the one in the posting.
    2. Check that the email domain matches the broker's company website. Free email addresses are a warning sign.
    3. A rate far above the market from a broker you do not know is a common bait.
    4. Never pay a fee to get a load, and confirm any change of payment details by phone.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I check a freight broker's MC number?

    Type the MC number into the field above. We look it up in the FMCSA licensing register and the public history of the broker's authority. For the full record, including insurance and the address on file, open the broker in FMCSA SAFER.

    What does carrier authority only mean?

    The company may haul freight as a carrier but has no active authority to arrange freight as a broker. A carrier that posts loads and hands them to other carriers without broker authority may be double brokering.

    Why is a new broker authority a risk?

    Many fraud schemes use a fresh authority or a bought one. We flag authorities younger than 180 days, and younger than 90 days as high risk. A new broker is not a scammer by default, so verify the contacts and ask for references.

    What is a suspension or revocation event?

    FMCSA suspends or revokes an authority, for example, when the broker's 75,000 dollar surety bond or trust fund lapses. We count such events in the last 12 months. One event that was fixed quickly is common, several are a warning sign.

    Does this show if the broker pays on time?

    No. This page shows licensing data only. The LoadLens extension shows the DAT credit score and days to pay next to each load, and Pro adds what other carriers report about the broker.

    Data comes from public FMCSA datasets that are updated daily, and we cache it for up to 12 hours. A clean record does not prove that a broker is honest, and a flag does not prove fraud. This is not legal or financial advice.
